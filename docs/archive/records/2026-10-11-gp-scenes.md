## Implemented outcome and evidence

Lux now has a validated version-1 authored map with independent mood/palette RGB
and lift, six scene families, explicit role bindings, musical condition ranges,
compatible predecessors and bounded fade/dwell/confirmation/variation/repetition.

remembers sixteen arrangement identities and uses an eight-selection history.
It samples a bounded 4.5-second energy history for change interpretation, confirms
changes and completes normal fades before switching again. Harmonic relationships
modify supported fade duration, never key-to-colour or chord-triggered scene rules.

fresh calibration, programmer/release/Hold precedence and loss retention. The

consumer boundary. Map edits are atomic and revision checked; previews are owner

## Review findings and corrections

Early thin episodes failed source freshness after 2–4 seconds. Avoiding synthetic
catch-up bursts alone was insufficient. Lux now shares immutable retry JSON and
maps, avoids redundant validation/serialization during polls and removes an extra
Service transaction clone while preserving admission/freshness/retry semantics.
No freshness, queue, transport or authority bound was widened. A later run exposed
harness renewal revision contention; bounded stale-revision retry fixed that test.
The next reached real rendering and exposed read-only F7 being classified as a
write; navigation now has a regression while editing remains role-gated.

Review also corrected trend sampling/history for slow builds, made variation affect
actual RGB, separated arrangement identity admission from tentative label confidence,
retained omitted role values explicitly, narrowed grants safely, compacted oversized
replies and required full density/confidence eligibility before stable-scene restraint.
The earlier failing observations remain local; they are not counted as acceptance.
